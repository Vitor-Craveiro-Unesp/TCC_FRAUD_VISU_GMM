Phi measures both the direction and the strength of association between two dichotomous variables.

For this analysis:

F = 0 and M = 1

Therefore, a positive Phi indicates a tendency for equal binary codes to occur together, whereas a negative Phi indicates a tendency for opposite binary codes to occur together.

The sign depends on the coding orientation. The magnitude describes the strength of association.

The Chi-square test does not provide sufficient statistical evidence of association between SEND_GENDER_BE and TRANS_YEAR_BE.

Why is there 1 degree of freedom?

The contingency table has 2 rows (F and M) and 2 columns (2019 and 2020).

For a Chi-square test of independence:

df = (rows - 1) × (columns - 1)

df = (2 - 1) × (2 - 1)

df = 1

Once the row totals and column totals are fixed, only one cell can vary freely. The remaining three cell frequencies are then mathematically determined.

Cramer's V = 0.001127, indicating a very weak or negligible association between SEND_GENDER_BE and TRANS_YEAR_BE.

Cramer's V measures the magnitude of association between categorical variables. It ranges from 0 to 1 and does not indicate association direction.

For a 2 × 2 contingency table, Cramer's V is equal to the absolute value of Phi:

V = |Phi|

Phi retains the direction of association, whereas Cramer's V expresses association magnitude only.

Combined interpretation:

The Chi-square test evaluates whether statistical evidence of association exists.

Phi evaluates both the direction and the magnitude of the binary-binary association.

Cramer's V evaluates the magnitude of the categorical association without considering direction.

Because the dataset contains a very large number of observations, statistical significance can occur even when the practical magnitude of the association is small. Therefore, the Chi-square p-value should be interpreted together with Phi, Cramer's V, the contingency table and the conditional proportions.
